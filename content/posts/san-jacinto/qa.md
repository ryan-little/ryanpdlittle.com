# Mt San Jacinto via Deer Springs — Interview Questions

Answer these to write the post. Drop answers below each question.

## Questions to Answer

1. Why Deer Springs from Idyllwild instead of the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway, which would have cut 4,000+ feet of climbing off the day?

2. What's Idyllwild like as a town, and what was the drive up from San Diego like?

3. Walk me through the self-issue permit kiosk — where exactly is it, how early did you get there, any friction?

4. 6:47am start. What did the first hour on Deer Springs Trail look like, what was the forest like down low?

5. The trail climbs from 5,600ft to 10,834ft over roughly 9 miles. Where did you first notice the altitude?

6. The route goes up Deer Springs and down a different way. What was the loop like, and why that route over an out-and-back?

7. What did crossing 10,000 feet feel like in the body — was there a moment you registered it, or did it just happen?

8. Describe the summit: weather was fair, 75 degrees, light wind. Who else was up there, what were the views, how long did you stay?

9. 19.77 miles is a long day. Where did the body start to fall apart?

10. You ended with blood blisters on both feet and a tight left calf. What happened on the descent, and what did you do when you got back to the car?

11. Standing at 10,834 knowing Whitney is at 14,505 — what does that gap feel like now that you've experienced your first 10K summit?

12. Anything about the Deer Springs approach or the day that surprised you, that you hadn't read or expected?

## Photo Ideas

- The self-issue permit kiosk at Pine Crest Ave — sign or permit slip
- Trailhead or early Deer Springs Trail through pine forest at dawn, light still low
- Mid-climb looking up through trees where the trail steepens and forest changes character
- First moment treeline breaks and views open, valley or desert floor dropping away
- Summit sign or benchmark at 10,834ft
- View from summit toward Coachella Valley, Palm Springs, or the Salton Sea
- Feet or boots on descent, wear from 20 miles visible
- Idyllwild town or parking area as a bookend — grounding the reader before the climb
