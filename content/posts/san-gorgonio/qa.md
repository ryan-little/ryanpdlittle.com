## Questions to Answer

1. One week after Baldy, back to back big weekends. How did your legs feel at the trailhead that morning, fresh or still carrying something from the week before?

**A:** Legs felt fresh, wasn't carrying anything except the pack.

2. This is the highest and longest of the Six-Pack (11,499 ft, 18.37 miles). Did it feel like "the big one" going in, or just the next hike on the list?

**A:** Just felt like the next hike at this point, though he was excited to get to the top of the tallest mountain in Southern California.

3. The Vivian Creek Trail passes three established camps on the way up: Vivian Creek Camp, Halfway Camp, High Creek Camp. Did those function as mental checkpoints for you, or did they blur together? IMG_0025 at 7:33am looks like it could be right around the creek/camp area, does that look right?

**A:** The camps didn't mean too much walking by or through them. Doesn't know what Halfway Camp is named after, since there's no way that was actually halfway up San Gorgonio.

4. 6:52am start per Garmin, so a before-dawn drive up to Forest Falls, and IMG_0010 has you at the Vivian Creek trailhead sign in early light. What was that drive and the trailhead scene like?

**A:** Drive was fine, doesn't love waking up that early but has done it enough times now hiking in the LA area. Nice that there's no traffic at all that early, before the morning light really gets going. Forest Falls was also a new area of LA for him, which was interesting.

5. Your average heart rate was 130, noticeably lower than Baldy's 146 despite this being a bigger effort by distance and gain. Was that a deliberate pacing choice for the longer day, or just how the effort distributed itself?

**A:** Thinks the average is lower mostly because he took more breaks and was generally a bit slower on the ascent, since it was steep right after the Mill Creek crossing and again right at the end for the summit push. Also likely lower because the descent took so long, dragging the average down with it.

6. You were stopped for about 48 minutes total (7h57m vs 7h9m moving), more than Baldy's 35. Longer summit stay, more breaks on the way up, something with the trail itself?

**A:** Mostly the summit stop again, but also for sure had a number of breaks on the ascent, more than usual for him.

7. This is the highest point you've reached yet, above Jacinto's 10,834 and Baldy's 10,064. Did the altitude announce itself differently at 11,500 feet, or did the previous climbs prepare you for it?

**A:** The prior climbs prepped him well, didn't feel too different from Jacinto or Baldy.

8. IMG_0068, 0073, and 0077 all show a rocky, boulder-strewn summit area with a sweeping panorama, no single dramatic high point the way Jacinto's boulder pile or Baldy's plaque marks it. Is that an accurate read on what the top of Gorgonio looks like, broad and rocky rather than a single peak?

**A:** Accurate, San Gorgonio doesn't really come to a point. He actually thought another cluster of rocks nearby might have been higher than the marked summit, but figured it was worth trusting the trail, the signs, and the other people up there that he was at the actual summit.

9. IMG_0044 and IMG_0068 both show the same distinctive peak rising above a solid cloud layer filling the valley below. Do you know which peak that is? And was that cloud inversion something that built through the morning, or was it there from early on?

**A:** 100% San Jacinto, not Baldy. He likes taking photos of peaks he's done or wants to do from wherever he currently is. The clouds were filling the valley between the two mountains.

10. Your summit sign in IMG_0068 is a hand-painted wooden board, not an official metal plaque like Baldy's. Do you know if that's something SGWA (San Gorgonio Wilderness Association) volunteers maintain, or is it just something hikers have left up there over the years?

**A:** Thinks most of these signs are made by other hikers and just left at the summit. Some have artist tags on them, some don't.

11. Any specific moment that stands out the way the coyotes did on Jacinto or the crowd contrast did on Baldy? Wildlife, a person you talked to, a scare, a view that stopped you?

**A:** The top of Gorgonio was windy as hell that day, huge gusts, had to shelter himself just to take his break because he was freezing from the wind. Made it hard to even take his summit photos from getting blown around so much. On overall nature/scenery: still below San Jacinto's Deer Springs route for him, but above Baldy, and probably equal to or slightly above Cucamonga and Wilson.

Also, separately: passed three national forest rangers on the way up and again on the way down. Expected them to check his permit but they just waved him through. Didn't ask what they were up there for, but based on their pace and where he passed them, he didn't think they'd be able to summit and get back to the trailhead in daylight, and they didn't appear to have camping gear. Not sure if there's some kind of ranger setup at a campground up there or what.

12. Weather and trail conditions late June, any exposed sun-heavy sections, any snow left this high up? (Basecamp logged 54°F and fair skies.)

**A:** The top of the mountain, the last few miles, were pretty exposed. The rest was in and out but mostly covered. Weather was nice the whole way until the freezing wind right at the peak.

13. With Gorgonio done, how are you thinking about where you stand on the Whitney goal? This is the tallest thing you've climbed toward it so far, and Whitney is still roughly 3,000 feet higher.

**A:** Thinking the hike up Whitney will be enjoyable and nice, and he'll start disliking it once it's time to descend. This hike really felt like it took forever going from top to bottom. On all of these longer hikes, the ascent almost feels shorter than the descent to him, probably just tired legs. Maybe worth considering slowing down or taking more breaks so he doesn't feel as fatigued on the way down.

14. Physical toll after two big weekends back to back. How did the days after compare to your recovery from Baldy or Jacinto?

**A:** Was sore post-Gorgonio, but nothing too different from what he's felt after the other peaks. Excited to go back and give it another go in August, maybe pick an alternate summit route if one makes sense.
