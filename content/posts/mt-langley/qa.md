This slot was originally a blank "White Mountain Peak" stub for 7/24 — the real-world plan shifted back in early July (Langley became the 7/10-11 hike, White Mountain got deferred to August), so this is a fresh Q&A built off the actual hike.

**Basecamp stats (Garmin, "Mt Langley via New Army Pass and Cottonwood Lakes," 2026-07-11):** started 4:25am, 23.58 mi, 5,545 ft gain, 10h32m total (8h28m moving), avg HR 147, max HR 180, 4,562 cal, summit 14,044 ft, trailhead elevation ~9,992 ft, weather 63°F and fair, 34% humidity, light wind (4 mph).

**Confirmed via web search:** official Mt Langley elevation is 14,032 ft (Wikipedia, NAVD 88) — Garmin's 14,044 ft is a ~12 ft drift, negligible. It's the southernmost California fourteener. The trailhead is Horseshoe Meadow (~10,034 ft official, matches your ~9,992 ft GPS start almost exactly). New Army Pass is the longer-but-easier approach out of Cottonwood Lakes (vs. the steeper Old Army Pass); from the pass it's mostly cross-country/use-trail to a Class 1 summit push along cairns. Often called the easiest California 14er. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Langley), [Timberline Trails route notes](https://www.timberlinetrails.com/LangleyTrail.html).
