Back in March I [wrote a roadmap](/posts/whats-coming-spring-2026/) that opened with a confession: two weeks earlier the blog had one post and no plan, and suddenly it had fourteen posts outlined through May and a GitHub Action publishing them on schedule. Most of that actually happened. The [Primal Chase dev log](/posts/building-primal-chase-part-0/) ran all five parts. The fitness project turned into its own four-part series from [Claude-Fit](/posts/claude-fit-the-ai-fitness-app-that-didnt-need-ai/) through [Basecamp](/posts/basecamp-training-coach/). The [finance dashboard](/posts/personal-finance-dashboard-apps-script/) and [Claude Code](/posts/claude-code-as-a-dev-environment/) writeups went out, and the personal posts, hiking and San Diego FC and the trip reports, filled in around them. The one thing that changed without me announcing it was the cadence. I started out publishing twice a week, Tuesdays and Fridays, and quietly settled into once a week on Fridays, which is the pace I can actually hold with a full-time job and the projects themselves to build.

## What Didn't Ship

Two things I promised in March haven't happened. The **RF viewshed tool** and the **Learning Geospatial Python in Public** series were both supposed to start in late April or May, and neither has gotten past a draft. They are the posts I most want to write and the ones that keep losing to whatever is already further along, which is its own kind of signal about where my attention actually goes when I'm not forcing it.

I'll also own a smaller miss. The spring post listed MCP servers as part of how I use Claude Code, and then the [Claude Code post](/posts/claude-code-as-a-dev-environment/) I actually published argued for skipping MCP in favor of plain CLI tools. The roadmap previewed the opposite of the conclusion. That's what happens when you tease a post before you've finished thinking it through.

## The Whitney Build

The spine of the summer is the climb. I'm working toward a Mount Whitney summit on August 23rd, and the training for it is a list of peaks I've been moving through in order, each one higher and longer than the last. The [Whitney Goal post](/posts/whitney-goal/) lays out the whole progression, and the [Mount Wilson post](/posts/mount-wilson/) was the first of the Six-Pack of Peaks that builds the altitude.

Cucamonga and Ontario Peak are both already done — same hike, same day — and so is San Jacinto from last week, and both writeups are in the queue. What's left in the Six-Pack is San Bernardino Peak, Baldy, and San Gorgonio, and each one gets its own post the way Wilson did, GPS maps and elevation profiles and whatever the day turned out to be. Gorgonio is the last big test before the permit date, and if it goes cleanly Whitney becomes a shorter, higher version of something I've already done. The summit attempt itself will get a post regardless of how it goes, because the honest version of a goal like this includes the outcome you didn't plan for as much as the one you did.

Running threads through all of it on a parallel track, building toward a half marathon distance this month and climbing from there. It mostly shows up in the hiking posts as the thing keeping my aerobic base under the climbs rather than as its own writeup, at least for now.

## The Rest

The GIS posts are still on the list. I'm not going to pin a date to them again, because the last time I did that the date came and went and the only thing it produced was this paragraph. When the wildfire dashboard has something real to show, the geospatial series starts, and the RF tool will follow the same rule. Progress first, then the post.

Everything else stays the same. Fridays, one post, written ahead when I have momentum and let the schedule handle the rest. The list in my terminal has a longer checked column than it did in March, and the climb at the top of it is finally close enough to plan around.
